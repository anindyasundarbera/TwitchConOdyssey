# S1 systems evidence and candidate profile

Research only; retrieved **2026-11-01**. This note proposes selections for D-01–D-05 in [GAMEPLAN-v2](../GAMEPLAN-v2.md) and [its data](../gameplan-v2.json). It approves no mechanic, integration or gameplay execution. All open selections still require human approval and a separate simulation authorization.

## Search boundary and evidence weight

Targeted English-language web search covered Microsoft Xbox Accessibility Guidelines (XAG), W3C accessibility guidance, Python clock documentation, original distributed-systems papers and original public-goods experiments. Queries combined input/objective clarity/difficulty, pause/monotonic time, event ordering, Sybil identity and reward/punishment cooperation. Publication years were unrestricted through retrieval; historical theory was retained where relevant. Included nine sources below; excluded roundups, secondary blogs, lecture summaries, repeated copies and other games' tuning numbers. This is a bounded narrative review, not an exhaustive systematic review; no runtime, provider or gameplay was exercised.

| Source and explicit evidence weight | Supported principle | Limitation for Odyssey |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Microsoft XAG 107: Input](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/107), official platform guidance; high relevance, normative practice rather than experiment | Prefer simple digital alternatives; avoid essential simultaneous inputs, rapid repetition and prolonged holds. | Does not establish that a particular lane count or transition speed is accessible; Microsoft is a platform stakeholder. |
| [Microsoft XAG 109: Objective clarity](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/109), official guidance; high relevance, same guidance family as XAG 107, not independent replication | Persistent clear objectives/progress reduce memory demands; suppress unrelated interruptions. | No experimental proof of stream comprehension or a validated HUD density. |
| [Microsoft XAG 108: Difficulty](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/108), official guidance; moderate relevance, same source family | Adjust challenge and avoid making progress depend on inaccessible physical or timing demands. | Supports conservative challenge design, not a specific warning, damage or recovery value; a minimal slice is not full XAG conformance. |
| [W3C SC 2.2.2 explanation](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide), official informative companion to a standard; high relevance to presentation | Allow control of distracting parallel moving/updating information, subject to essential-activity exceptions. | Not a game simulation pause specification; does not validate this match timer or establish WCAG conformance. |
| [Python `time.monotonic_ns`](https://docs.python.org/3.13/library/time.html#time.monotonic_ns), official implementation documentation; high weight for API semantics | Use differences from a monotonic clock, unaffected by system-clock updates. | Monotonicity alone supplies neither deterministic replay nor pause subtraction, scheduler bounds or suspend behavior. |
| [Lamport, *Time, Clocks, and the Ordering of Events* (1978)](https://lamport.azurewebsites.net/pubs/time-clocks.pdf), original peer-reviewed theoretical paper; high weight within assumptions | Distinguish causal order from an explicitly constructed total order; deterministic tie ordering must be specified. | Does not prescribe crowd-vote fairness or prove this engine correct; distributed consensus is unnecessary for a single writer. |
| [Douceur, *The Sybil Attack* (2002)](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2002/01/IPTPS2002.pdf), original workshop theory; high weight for identity limits | Multiple identities can defeat redundancy and apparent participant counts. | Authenticated account identity is not unique-human assurance; no K/quorum formula repairs that gap. |
| [Rand et al., *Positive Interactions Promote Public Cooperation* (2009)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2875121/), original peer-reviewed laboratory experiment; moderate weight for incentives, low transfer confidence | In repeated public-goods groups, reward maintained cooperation with better earnings than costly punishment. | Persistent laboratory groups and real experimental payoffs differ from transient viewers and fictional credits; no support for this split or danger multiplier. |
| [Herrmann et al., *Antisocial Punishment Across Societies* (2008)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18323447/), original peer-reviewed multi-pool experiment, abstract reviewed; moderate weight | Punishment can target cooperators and undermine cooperation; avoid audience-controlled personal sanctions. | Context varied substantially; not direct evidence about livestream griefing or a universal argument against penalties. |

The reward papers are independent experiments, not replicas of the same intervention. Their contexts limit any apparent consensus: positive reciprocity can help, while sanctions can backfire. Confidence is high in documented clock semantics, moderate in the presentation principles, and low in transfer to audience behavior. No direct evidence found validates Odyssey's anti-stun caps, warning interval, quorum, or bounty formula. Safety below is a proposed invariant to verify later, not an empirical claim.

## One compact candidate profile

**Every numeric gameplay value below is a project design inference, not externally validated.** The existing user-proposed cadence is preserved but remains untested. No extra hazards, counters, upgrades, checkpoints, leaderboards, vetoes, punishment tools or operator powers are proposed.

**D-01 — controls and presentation.** Automatic forward travel; **3 lanes**. Remappable left/right digital actions, initially arrow keys and gamepad D-pad; **1 press** requests **1 adjacent lane** transition over **0.5 s**; held keys do not repeat. Opposite inputs in the same engine step cancel. No acceleration, jumping or button combinations. During planning and resolution, steering remains active. The proposed interpretation of “locked input” is **crowd/counter input locks**, not streamer steering; this resolves an ambiguity, requires explicit approval, and must not silently rewrite the plan. No manual counter confirmation/override; default abstention. HUD retains progress, phase/hazard timer and one text/icon consequence preview; no chat-reading requirement or color-only warning.

**D-02 — one bounded obstacle.** At most **1 obstacle per turn**, **3 per match**, fixed center-lane placement, never overlapping. Warning occupies the last **5 s** of planning and names the blocked lane and safe adjacent lanes. Uncountered obstacle lasts the first **3 s** of resolution. A collision applies **20% forward-speed reduction for 2 s**; **0 HP damage**, **0 stun**, **0 steering loss**, no displacement. Each obstacle can affect the avatar only once, then becomes inert; cooldown prevents another obstacle until the next resolution (**30 s** between activations). Caps: **2 s slowdown per turn**, **6 s per match**, **0.4 s equivalent travel loss per turn**, **1.2 s per match**. No stacking, refresh or residual effect into the next turn. `clear_lane` removes the obstacle before collision evaluation. Missing quorum takes the bounded uncountered branch. No HP system, death, respawn or checkpoints. Wipe means undelivered at the match deadline; **0 automatic retries**, no carried pool, fresh explicitly started match resets all state. Fixed safe lanes and warning make avoidance possible; later fixtures must check reachability from every admitted state.

**D-03 — identity and deterministic aggregation.** For a later live path, use authenticated platform subject, bound to both chat and button; reject unbound input. Offline subjects are labeled fixtures, never asserted humans. Freeze eligible account roster at planning start; joins/leaves take effect next turn, reconnect keeps identity. Candidate **K=2**, assigned alternately after stable subject sorting; squads are display groupings, not equal-weight electoral blocks. Each eligible account has weight **1**, total influence **1 per turn** across channels. First valid receipt wins atomically; **0 replacements**, retries return `duplicate`. Select `clear_lane` only with at least **ceil(0.60N)** roster votes and **1** actual eligible vote; otherwise abstain. Abstention wins ties, including an empty roster. Bots contribute **0** to quorum, votes and bounty. This prevents a minority of the frozen account roster selecting the counter; it cannot prevent participation suppression or Sybils and must not be advertised as human-level fairness.

**D-04 — fictional collective bounty.** Base **0 credits**. Each distinct accepted human-origin obstacle adds **20**, maximum **1 increment per turn**, pool cap **60**; linear escalation, multiplier **1**, no danger-author bonus. Countering/avoiding an accepted obstacle does not remove its increment, so actual harm has no extra reward. Delivery settles **50% streamer / 50% participating audience**; any collision halves total settlement, once, before splitting; wipe/build-drop settles **0**. Audience eligibility requires at least **1 accepted counter vote** that match; danger creation alone earns no audience allocation. Divide audience credits equally as exact rational ledger shares; no rounding redistribution or popularity weights. With no eligible audience, its share is withheld. Bots/fixtures create **0** increments and earn **0** live credits; a fixture demonstration ledger stays explicitly synthetic. Residual incentive to arrange safe danger remains; caps limit farming but do not prove grief resistance.

**D-05 — clock and boundaries.** Match deadline **90 s active simulation time**; free-route traversal target **75 s**, leaving headroom above the proposed slowdown cap. Cadence **20/5/5 s**, at most **3 turns**. One monotonic host clock, integer nanoseconds and one authoritative sequence counter; replay uses recorded logical times/sequence, not wall time. Planning interval is half-open: receipts at **t=20 s** expire. Same-time ordering: pause/deadline/phase-close boundaries first, then commands by authority-assigned sequence, then effects/settlement. AI timeout **2 s** from dispatch or planning close, whichever is earlier; **0 automatic retries**, late result discarded, never rolled over. Post-cutoff grace **0 ms** for both transport and model results; client timestamps cannot backdate eligibility. Pause freezes phase, hazard, slowdown and match clocks, rejects commands while paused, invalidates pending model work; resume resets the scheduling anchor with **0 catch-up ticks**. Pause duration is unlimited and visibly distinct from active match time; no claim of a real-world deadline. Delivery must precede deadline strictly; equality wipes. These are proposed boundary oracles, not observed timing behavior.
